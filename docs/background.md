# Clinical & Pharmacogenomic Background: CYP2D6

Cytochrome P450 2D6 (*CYP2D6*) is a polymorphic hepatic enzyme whose genotype-predicted metabolizer phenotype—poor (PM), intermediate (IM), normal (NM), or ultrarapid (UM)—is assigned using the CPIC activity-score system across inherited star alleles.

## Psychiatric Drug Relevance (CPIC & DPWG Guidelines)
* **SSRIs and SNRIs (CPIC, 2023):** CYP2D6 extensively metabolises **paroxetine, fluvoxamine, venlafaxine, and vortioxetine**. CPIC provides genotype-guided prescribing recommendations—including dose reductions or selecting an alternative agent not predominantly metabolised by CYP2D6—for PMs and UMs (and lower starting doses/slower titration for paroxetine IMs) to reduce side-effect risk or treatment failure. By contrast, CPIC notes little to no effect of *CYP2D6* variation on sertraline exposure (which is guided primarily by *CYP2C19* and *CYP2B6*).
* **Tricyclic Antidepressants (CPIC, 2017):** CYP2D6 hydroxylates **amitriptyline, nortriptyline**, and related tricyclics (clomipramine, desipramine, doxepin, imipramine, trimipramine) to less active metabolites. For depression dosing, CPIC recommends avoiding tricyclics or reducing the starting dose by 50% (with therapeutic drug monitoring) in PMs, considering a 25% reduction in IMs, and avoiding tricyclics due to subtherapeutic levels in UMs.
* **Antipsychotics (DPWG, 2023):** The Dutch Pharmacogenetics Working Group (DPWG) provides actionable *CYP2D6* gene–drug dosing recommendations for **aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, haloperidol, pimozide, risperidone, and zuclopenthixol**—recommending dose reductions in PMs (and selected IMs) to mitigate adverse effects such as extrapyramidal symptoms or QT prolongation, and alternative agents or dose increases in UMs. DPWG concludes that *CYP2D6* genotype does not require therapy adjustment for clozapine.

## Population Context & Future Work
Indian cohort studies (e.g., Naveen et al., 2006; Manoharan et al., 2019) show that South Asian *CYP2D6* allele distributions differ notably from European and East Asian reference populations, with reduced-function alleles such as `*41` and `*10` playing a major role. This atlas relies solely on publicly accessible summary resources (PharmVar, ClinPGx/CPIC, published Indian cohort literature, and supplementary gnomAD v4 calls). Controlled-access Indian genomic cohorts such as **GenomeIndia** were not accessed here and remain a priority for future work to refine region-specific allele frequencies and structural-variant prevalence.
